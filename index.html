Part 1: Milestone 1

 

 

 

This is the Sonic Solar System! It has 8 planets corresponding to 8 equally spaced frequency bins through the range of frequencies that humans can hear. The size of the planet changes based on the amplitude of the frequencies within each bin. The sound waveform is on the top.

I was worried that the sonic solar system was a little too complex of an idea. However, I was surprised to see how far I got using the sndpk.ck and orbit.ck reference codes! I also found that in the process of understanding this code helped clarify again some concepts for me from MUS 320. I felt very excited to be able to work through this as a designer. Up till this point, I honestly felt like i was just trying to make the program work in the first place without putting too much pressure and stress to fine tune the program. However, I'm still very happy with how this turned out. I channeled some of that wonder and excitement I felt watching space documentaries when I was younger. Next steps would include further refining the scale of the planets, potentially changing the color of the planets based on which frequencies are dominant within a certain bin, making the speed of the orbit correspond to something, and refining how I create the different bins. Having more concerte bins also almost gamifies the visualizer so that the user tries to make certain planets bigger or smaller by changing their voice?

 

 Part 0 - Brainstorm

I have played around with several of the example code snippets on the ChuGL website and had a lot of fun with the sound waveform, spectrum, and waterfall plots. Below are my ideas for the sound visualizer. They tend to be a bit ambitious but I will make sure to figure out a more reasonable version for the project.

 

  • Sonic Solar System – I found myself unexpectedly moved by orbit.ck – I’ve always loved space documentaries growing up and the feeling of expansiveness and smallness at the same time moves me a lot. I was thinking of having the waveform in the sun object in the center, and for each frequency, have an planet generated and make one or a few orbits. The speed of the orbit can be inversely correlated to the amplitude of that particularly frequency in the spectrum. The distance from the sun can be the frequency. There can be a spectrum of colors red to purple where red

represents lower frequencies and purple represents higher frequencies.

 

  • Sonic Stars – again going with the space theme, there can be a space shuttle picture in the middle and the waveform along the space shuttle. The background is a bunch of stars! The position of the stars can be determined by the frequency and amplitude of all the frequencies in the spectrum – frequency and amplitude can correlate to x and y axis position. When the microphone picks up sound, the stars appear and can stay on the screen for a couple seconds! Maybe there can also be a way to put a twinkle sound effect somehow – haven’t figured out how that may work.

 

  • Headphone? More like Head Frequency – This could be a small animation where there are two circles represented ends of a headphone and an arc connecting the two circles. The sound waveform will be visualized in the center. There will be curved lines emitted from each ear phone with the color, speed, and thickness of the lines corresponding to the various frequencies and their amplitudes in the spectrum.
